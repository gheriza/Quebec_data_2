                   PRELIMINARY REPORT ON HURRICANE CLEO
                            AUGUST 14-19, 1958


The existence of Hurricane "Cleo" in the Atlantic some 900 miles east of the Antilles (near 14.6N, 47.1W) was
confirmed by Air Force reconnaissance aircraft in the afternoon of Thursday, August 14, 1958.  By this time it
had already reached full hurricane force, with strongest winds estimated at 145 mph, a central pressure of
28.mbs, and an apparent movement toward the west northwest at 17 mph.  Reports from surface vessels during
the previous two or three days had indicated the presence of a disturbance to the east of this location, but not
enough information was available to confirm it until reconnaissance aircraft flew into the area.

The hurricane warning center at San Juan, Puerto Rico issued the first hurricane advisory on Cleo at 6 p.m. AST
on August 14, announcing a Hurricane Watch for the Windward and Leeward Islands and warning vessels in the
path of this severe hurricane to exercise caution.

Further reconnaissance the next day (August 15) shoved the hurricane to be moving northwestward, and
subsequent reports and analysis indicated persistence of a northwest to north movement.  Hurricane Watch was
accordingly discontinued for the Leeward Islands the morning of August 16.  The strength of the hurricane
decreased very slowly as it moved on a northerly course but the highest winds did not drop below hurricane
force until after its course had curved to the northeast and crossed the 45th parallel August 19-20.  It
subsequently continued on eastward toward Europe as a severe storm.

Hurricane Cleo did not seriously affect any land area but it was a dangerous threat to Atlantic shipping
throughout its history as it traversed the principal transatlantic shipping lanes.  Ships at sea were kept informed
of its location, strength, and movement through the marine bulletins broadcast over Radio Station NSS
Washington.
