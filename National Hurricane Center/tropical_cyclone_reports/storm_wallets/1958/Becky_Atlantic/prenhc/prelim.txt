           PRELIMINARY REPORT ON TROPICAL STORM BECKY
                      AUGUST 11-15, 1958.



Existence of Tropical Storm "Becky" in the Atlantic Ocean midway between the
Lesser Antilles and the Cape Verde Islands was announced by the San Juan
Weather Bureau forecast center at 11:00 p.m. EST Monday, August 1.1, 1958. 
Strongest winds around the center were then 50 miles per hour, and the storm
was moving westward about 1,8 miles per hour.  At noon the next day (Tuesday)
with the aid of Air Force reconnaissance the center was fixed at 17.5 N, 51.0
W, and it was moving westward at 17 mph.  Subsequently the forward motion
increased to about 25 mph but the storm did not intensify appreciably. 
Highest winds were estimated about C)o mph on August 13 and 14.  It passed
well to the north of the Antilles and northeast of the Bahamas, curving
northward between Florida and Bermuda on August 15 and then moved
northeastward along the Middle Atlantic and New England coasts.

At the time of this preliminary report there appears to have been no serious
loss or damage from Tropical Storm Becky.  However, high waves and surf along
the Atlantic coast August 17 and 18 appears to have been caused by the remains
of the storm as it moved northeastward offshore.
